    How the mesh is built

    The profile below is the one the 3D view revolves: a 1.5·Rt converging arc, a 0.382·Rt throat arc, and a quadratic Bézier bell from N to E with its middle point Q at the tangent intersection. Kit pieces are placed on a ring around the chamber and scale with pump power.

      Seven cycles, one part

      Each cycle is a data node with its own Pc range, pump pressure rise, mass factor, ignition rules and kit. These renders come from the same generator as the designer, using a real-engine-like preset for each.

      What this mockup simplifies

      Approximations

      The numbers follow the DESIGN.md model closely enough to show the trade-offs, but this page is a sketch of the mod, not the mod.

      Thermochemistry. Frozen-γ fits with c* by chamber pressure, tuned to the §5.2 sanity table. The mod would use CEA tables of Isp and Cf over Pc × ε, which reads 2–3 % higher for hydrolox and methalox.

      Separation. Summerfield criterion with k = 0.35: when exit pressure drops below 0.35 × ambient, Cf is evaluated at the wall point where that pressure is reached. No side-load or reliability model here.

      Mass. The §5.5 component sum with hand-tuned coefficients (Merlin ≈ 0.5 t, RL10 ≈ 0.16 t, RS-25 ≈ 3.2 t). Pressure-fed includes helium bottles, so it looks heavier than real engines that keep pressurant in the tank.

      Atmosphere. Standard Earth troposphere with an exponential above 11 km. The mod would read pressure from the launch body.

      Geometry. The bell, chamber and dome follow §8.2–§8.3. Kit pieces are primitives and swept tubes placed by simple rules; in the mod they get placement handles and template-vertex stretching.

      Plume. A shader cone whose width, length and shock-diamond spacing follow pe/pa. In the game this is a Waterfall template scaled by exit diameter (§9).

      ProcEngines is a planned mod. Design reference: _dev/ProceduralEngines/DESIGN.md. Real-engine names are comparisons only.
